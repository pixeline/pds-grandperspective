# Brief

## What was asked for

Not an avatar. An **instrument**: a generator anyone can run on their own handle. The
picture is only the first test of whether the mapping is any good.

Three answers set the frame:

- **Abstract data-portrait, no face.**
- **True over flattering.** Show what the data actually says, even if unflattering.
- **Whole repo** — every collection, including weird lexicons. Not just Bluesky posts.

## The graphic system

> "a graphic system that is mapped to the user's data content. principles of data
> visualization, mixed with the abstract art of Mondrian's Boogie Woogie, generative art,
> glitch art (as i see it, the exploration of the aesthetics of errors), etc. an
> inspiration could be a kaleidoscope where color, shape, brightness are generated from
> data passed through a system of parameters (as coined by LAb[au]'s metadesign)."

Four things follow from this, and one thing had to be resolved against it.

**Mondrian, *Broadway Boogie Woogie*.** The grid is syncopated, not regular. Its lines are
built from runs of small alternating colour blocks, and the blocks sit at intersections
rather than in the middle of fields. This is the direct source for how transitions are
drawn.

**Glitch as the aesthetics of error.** Taken literally: the glitch must come from actual
invalid records in the repo, never from a filter applied for texture. Simulated error is
the same dishonesty as hash-seeded noise, just in a different costume.

**LAb[au], metadesign.** The designer designs the generative system; the artefact is one
instantiation of it. Consequence: the parameter panel, the measurements, and the legend are
part of the work. They are not an explanatory footnote attached to a picture.

**Kaleidoscope — rejected, with reason.** A kaleidoscope and Boogie Woogie want opposite
things. Mondrian is asymmetric and off-beat; a kaleidoscope is n-fold radial symmetry.
Symmetry also destroys information: mirror a signal four times and you have shown one
quarter of it, four times over. Since the subject of this piece is the passage between
states, and symmetry replaces passages with repetitions, asymmetry wins. The parametric
generation of colour, shape and brightness from data — the part of the kaleidoscope
reference that mattered — is kept in full.

## Time, and Marin Kasimir

On what the temporal axis means:

> "lexicon = the space where i like to dwell. Time defines 'me', now being who I am, and
> every passing second that is less and less representative of who I am, for I have
> changed. it makes me think of this artist Marin Kasimir who represents the states
> in-between subjects as the subject."

References: `marinkasimir.com/portfolio-item/rectangular/` — the work sits under **Stills &
Frames**, part of a project called **From here to there**. Frames cut out of transit.

Two design consequences:

1. **The mark is not the record. It is the transition.** A like, then a post, then a custom
   record, then nine hours of nothing. That sequence of switches and silences is the
   in-between-as-subject, and it is fully computable from a repo.
2. **Decay, not a rolling window.** Older records keep their position and lose saturation.
   The past stays visible as residue. Stitched-panorama logic rather than forgetting.

## Format

> "a square, when viewed frontal, but can be rotated and explored as a pile of tiles,
> perhaps even with a rotation angle that could be one parameter (like a DNA spiral chain)"

This resolves the square-versus-rectangle conflict instead of picking a side. The square is
not the object; it is the **frontal projection** of the object. Head-on, all history
collapses into one flat plate — a legitimate square avatar. Rotate it and the plate turns
out to have been a stack all along.

Because a collection holds the same x/y region in every tile, dwelling in it becomes a
**vein running through the depth** of the stack, visible only obliquely. Frontally the
piece reads as a Mondrian field; obliquely as sedimentary layers. Same data, two grammars.

The twist accumulates tile over tile, giving the helix.

## Interface decisions

- **Interactive orbit, no autoplay.** The verb was *explored*. Autonomous drift would be
  the piece performing at the viewer instead of the viewer turning it over.
- **The view angle is a displayed value.** Frontal is the avatar; oblique is the person.
  Making that a readout rather than a hidden state is the thesis of the piece, so the
  interface names which one you are looking at.
- **All saturated colour is reserved for data.** The chrome gets none — pale cool ground,
  grotesque in tight uppercase for labels, monospace for every number. An instrument, not
  a gallery.
